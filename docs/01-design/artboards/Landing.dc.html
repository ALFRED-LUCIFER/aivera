From job description to a decision you can defend

  1. 1

    Agree what matters

    Paste the job description. Aivera drafts five or six competencies with weights and the evidence that would prove each. Nothing is assessed until the hiring manager approves.

  2. 2

    Collect the work

    Repositories, test suites, design files, documents, shipped products. Where a candidate has nothing to show, Aivera asks for a short work sample and labels it as one.

  3. 3

    See why each person ranks where they do

    Every score cites its source and says how strong the evidence is. Claims are marked as claims, and count for nothing until something supports them.

  4. 4

    Interview only what is unproven

    Each interview gets questions tied to the gaps in that candidate’s evidence. Scorecards move the score, with the reason recorded.

  5. 5

    Decide with the pack in front of you

    Strengths, risks and open questions for each shortlisted candidate, on one page. Aivera recommends. You decide.

Not only software engineers

AI is changing what good looks like in testing, data, operations, product, design and customer-facing engineering too. Each role family has its own kinds of proof, and Aivera knows which to look for.

No public work? Many testers, analysts and support specialists have none. Aivera requests a short work sample scoped to the role instead.

Tell us the role you need

Recommendations you can check. Decisions that stay yours.

Every score cites its source

Open any assessment and see the exact commit, document or reference it rests on, and what it does not show.

Claims are labelled as claims

Nothing is called verified unless a second source confirms it. Candidate-submitted, corroborated and contradictory evidence look different.

Nobody is rejected automatically

Ranking orders your attention. Lower-ranked candidates stay visible, and thin evidence is treated as a gap to close.

Numbers say what they are

Observed, baseline or projection. If no hire has completed, the dashboard says so instead of showing a figure.

One number to judge us on: interviews per completed hire

Run a pilot on one role. It passes if you hold 30% fewer interviews per hire than your own baseline. You bring the baseline; Aivera shows the working.
